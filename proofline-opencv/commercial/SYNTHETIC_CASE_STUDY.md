# Synthetic case study — visual review pilot

**SYNTHETIC ONLY. No customer, production result, observed saving, acceptance, deployment, or revenue is represented here.**

A fictional manufacturer has 5,000 units per monthly period. The owner-entered scenario assumes 45 seconds of manual visual review per unit, a loaded reviewer cost of $42/hour, and asks what the arithmetic would look like **if** a ProofLine-assisted workflow routed 20% of units to human review.

Under those assumptions, the compiler calculates a baseline manual-review labor scenario of $2,625 per period and a scoped-review scenario of $525 per period, a modeled delta of $2,100 per period / $25,200 per year. This is not an observed saving, forecast, guarantee, accounting result, or defect-cost estimate.

The pilot hypothesis is to test a narrower question: can a buyer-approved reference generation and inspection frame reproducibly produce receipt-bound visual-change evidence, with detected regions routed to human review while every product-disposition, payment and outbound authority bit remains false?

The sample carrier uses a $12,500 fixed pilot hypothesis marked `PROPOSED_NOT_ACCEPTED`. There is no payment link and no claim that any buyer has seen or accepted this price.
