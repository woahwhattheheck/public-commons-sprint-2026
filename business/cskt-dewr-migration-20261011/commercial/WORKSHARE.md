# CSKT DEWR content and GIS migration workshare

## A complete technical delivery for the prime’s website project

TokenJunkieLabs proposes to configure and deliver the content, document and GIS migration workstream for Orca Design Group’s CSKT DEWR website team. The maintained application gives staff a complete source inventory, explicit migration decisions, named review, recoverable WordPress delivery and a continuing operator workflow. Orca leads the customer relationship, visual design, branding, institutional website architecture and overall bid. DEWR reviews its content and retains its accounts and administrative control.

**Fixed implementation fee: USD 8,400. Delivery: ten business days after the agreed source, environment, design interfaces and reviewers are available. Payment: USD 4,200 at kickoff and USD 4,200 at accepted delivery and handover.**

The original DEWR RFP schedules proposals for October 29, 2026 at 4:30 p.m. MDT and anticipates project kickoff in January 2027. The ten-day workshare is an implementation schedule inside the prime’s agreed project plan. [RFP §1.2, page 4](https://csktwatercompact.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/DEWR_Website_RFP_Final.pdf)

## Included cohort and operating scope

One source website and one agreed WordPress destination; up to **40 approved source pages, 400 media or document records, and four ArcGIS application references**. These limits cover the current public capture: 33 pages and 353 distinct media objects, comprising 130 PDF, 3 DOCX, 2 ZIP, 201 image and 17 video records. The current API’s total header reports 359; repeated complete pagination returns the same 353 actual IDs. Inventory decisions use those actual returned records.

The engagement includes two consolidated review/correction cycles and one approved technical release to the destination. Source media files come from the approved original export or transfer location; copied files retain their identity and source history. The approved inventory identifies large videos, external documents and any source objects that need a separate transfer window.

Transfer capacity is confirmed at intake. The native destination supports each selected original file up to 1 GiB. In the October 11 source capture, 335 of the 353 media records supply native file sizes totaling 1,168,620,882 bytes; the largest reported file is a 96,394,065-byte ZIP. The 17 videos account for 295,901,042 bytes, with a 34,109,195-byte maximum. These are original WordPress metadata values. The receiving team confirms the remaining 18 file sizes, the actual transferred bytes, generated image variants and backup space against its destination capacity. | Work product | What the receiving team can operate |
|---|---|

Three original StoryMaps are accessible. The fourth original reference is a legacy MapSeries URL that needs a current replacement or approved fallback. The scope includes resolving its page integration with DEWR’s supplied current app; creating a new GIS dataset, map or StoryMap is separately scoped.

## Inputs and responsibilities

At kickoff, the prime supplies the approved original WordPress/content export, source files, destination staging access, agreed theme or page-template interfaces, current ArcGIS item URLs and permissions, and one named coordinator who consolidates DEWR decisions. Existing hosting and platform subscriptions remain in the customer’s accounts.

The first review confirms the content taxonomy, sensitive or retired material, source-to-target routes, media transfer plan, approved GIS fallback text, staff roles, and the applicable accessibility standard for this workstream. The RFP requests the current accepted accessibility standard without naming a numbered conformance level; the agreed acceptance record identifies the actual standard and scope. [RFP §§4.4, 5.1, 5.3, pages 9–11](https://csktwatercompact.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/DEWR_Website_RFP_Final.pdf)

DEWR supplies and approves substantive text, public statements, Tribal identity, maps and media. Included formatting covers heading structure, meaningful link labels, approved alternative text, document metadata, consistent templates and accessible fallback presentation. The scope incorporates approved copy edits during the two review cycles. Substantive rewriting, PDF/DOCX remediation and new multimedia are itemized separately below, as requested in RFP §§4.4 and 4.6.

## Delivery sequence and acceptance

**Days 1–2:** confirm inputs and roles; reconcile the full selected source inventory; agree disposition, categories, routes, GIS references and the receiving environment.

**Days 3–5:** configure the original review application and destination integration; load the complete cohort; prepare content/document views and GIS fallback pages; produce the first reviewed staging release.

**Days 6–8:** operate both consolidated review/correction cycles with the prime and designated DEWR reviewer. Inspect destination pages, document links, routes, role behavior and the four GIS integrations. Correct implementation findings within the agreed scope.

**Days 9–10:** complete the selected release, receiving-operator walkthrough, restart/recovery exercise and source/configuration/admin handover.

The prime accepts when every selected source record has an agreed disposition; approved records and routes are present at the destination; each selected GIS reference has its agreed functioning integration or approved accessible fallback; staff can perform the review/edit/release workflow; and the original target state, release package and workspace can be recovered using the supplied instructions. A discrepancy list identifies any source content that DEWR elects to retire or carry into a later editorial phase. See [ACCEPTANCE.md](ACCEPTANCE.md) for the receiving sequence.

Review dates assume consolidated decisions arrive within one business day of the agreed review session. A delay in content approval, access, platform availability or the prime’s design interfaces moves the corresponding delivery date; the affected work and revised date are recorded together.

## Warranty and support

The fixed fee includes **30 calendar days of implementation warranty after technical handover**. It covers defects attributable to the delivered migration/configuration/integration work against the accepted scope. Reported loss of the delivered publishing workflow or an implementation-caused public integration failure receives an initial response within **one business day**; other implementation issues receive an initial response within **two business days**. The response includes a diagnosis or a correction plan and an agreed correction date.

Support is provided remotely, Monday through Friday excluding U.S. federal holidays. The prime remains the public website’s service contact and coordinates customer communication. The receiving plan records the working time zone and escalation contacts.

**Optional continuing technical support: USD 600 per month for up to four engineering hours.** This covers operation of the delivered migration/integration modules, correction of approved mappings, GIS link changes, a monthly retained-release review and a minor approved content/configuration update. Additional authorized work is USD 150 per hour. Unused hours do not roll over; either party may end the monthly service before the next billing period. The original source, accepted releases and customer accounts remain with the customer.

## Ownership and complete handover

The delivery contains the maintained source, destination integration, editable configuration, source inventory, content/route decisions, accepted release, original target recovery material, staff guide and technical operating instructions. CSKT retains its domain, hosting, analytics, CMS, ArcGIS and other applicable accounts and receives the administrative access required to operate the delivered work. Third-party licenses remain explicitly identified. [RFP §5.13, pages 12–13](https://csktwatercompact.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/DEWR_Website_RFP_Final.pdf)

The offer is a complete technical subcontract workstream within the prime’s institutional website redesign. The prime’s design/brand deliverables, procurement submission, reference declarations, forms/newsletter integrations, broader website hosting and customer approvals remain in its overall project plan.
