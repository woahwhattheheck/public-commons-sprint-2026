Method, original editions, and practical use

A transparent review order

  1. Select the whole retained domain, a utility's original focus watersheds, or its deduplicated in-domain upstream union.
  2. Compare absolute hectares or hectares per 1,000 classified hectares. Gross developed gain, forest loss, and wetland loss retain their original values. No loss or gain is substituted by net change.
  3. Positive values receive tied midranks on the full 217-watershed reference; zero gross change receives zero. The displayed preference score is the weighted average of those scales.
  4. The sensitivity grid exhausts all three nonnegative weights that sum to ten: 66 choices in ten-percentage-point steps, including single-criterion endpoints. Rank range and top-K counts describe these choices, not probabilities, uncertainty intervals or model calibration.
  5. The trade-off front contains watersheds not dominated on all three criteria within the current view. Reviewers can override rank, record a question and queue any watershed.

Ranking totals are not investment returns, pollutant loads or project effectiveness. A single classification transition can increase more than one screening criterion. The editable lens makes those preferences visible rather than treating them as independent impacts.

Read the source correctly

Annual classifications compare 2023 with 2025 on the original common raster support. A mapped class difference is not an observed event date or a measured causal effect. Developed classes include open space; developed area is not fractional imperviousness. Forest and woody-wetland groups remain separate.

A station or gauge is joined by its original point location. A local gauge is not automatically representative of the whole watershed or its upstream union. “Neither” refers only to these retained inventories. The 2026 assessment is a draft edition, and listed causes require their own relevant source interpretation.

ARJWS and PRWD are two utilities with four original focus HUC12 watersheds. Their source-based upstream unions are whole watersheds within the retained 217-HUC domain, not engineered intake contributing areas or a South Carolina-only reporting mask.
