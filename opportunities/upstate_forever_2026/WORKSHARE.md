# Ecosystem Sciences × TokenJunkie Labs — proposed Upper Savannah workshare

**Prepared for internal commercial use, October 12, 2026. Proposed fee: USD18,600.**

## The useful result

Give Ecosystem Sciences a complete, repeatable source-to-GIS production workflow for its proposed Upper Savannah engagement: retained public and supplied inputs, an editable scientific operator, the original InVEST NDR/SDR runs, explicit land-cover alternatives, seven map families, physical export rasters, parcel priorities, metadata, an understandable report and the full working project that its own operator can reopen.

This expands the original October 11 paid-QA inquiry into a substantive engineering subcontract. The published application now covers the working study and its receiving path. The earlier inquiry contained no price; USD18,600 is the new proposed scope and fee.

### 2. Scientific review, observations and parameter selection

TokenJunkie Labs prepares the parameter/source register and executes the agreed comparisons. Ecosystem Sciences determines model suitability, applicable regional coefficient lineage, local adjustment, calibration objective, validation design and the interpretation communicated to UF. A transferred literature profile is recorded as a regional reference; a locally fitted profile records its actual calibration inputs and objective. The retained [source methods](source/SOURCE_METHODS.md) and [observation workflow](operator/OBSERVATIONS.md) supply the existing implementations.

For each candidate observation, engineering prepares one source-linked record containing the original station/catchment identity, constituent, measurement or load unit, observation period, method, qualifier and uncertainty. Annual model-to-observation comparisons use compatible annual N/P/sediment loads and the **complete contributing catchment**, including the modeled upstream area. The workbench records raster coverage, expected structural stream omissions and other missing support before interpreting a residual. Concentrations, bacterial measurements and cyanotoxins stay available in the review with their original measurement definitions.

The prime's scientific review settles:

- Which observations can inform the chosen model and period; whether retained public observations or supplied utility data support local adjustment.
- Which records inform parameter choice and which independently assess it, including any repeated catchments, common estimators, reservoir influence or point-source contribution.
- The constituent-specific objective and parameter interpretation, with the baseline/scenario production profile selected before final scenario comparisons.
- How the observed pollutant and contaminant evidence informs conservation recommendations and the explanation of sediment/nutrient pathways.

Engineering produces the retained observation comparison, coverage and residual tables, method notes and the chosen profile edition. Existing programme and paired-ensemble tools can reuse completed cases to examine profile sensitivity, with explicit cohort members and support. The production release reports the selected model's quantities and the uncertainty that its actual sources and comparisons support. Calibration and sensitivity are consultant method choices under this plan; the issuer does not prescribe a named fitting method or a fixed number of simulations. [RFP, pages 1–2 and appendix pages 8–9](https://www.upstateforever.org/files/files/20261002_FINAL_EC-SDC%20Mapping%20RFP%20APPENDIX%20COMBINED.pdf#page=8)

### 3. Baseline, two alternatives and quantitative priorities

The included three-run production cycle uses one current-condition baseline and two prime-reviewed land-cover alternatives on the same declared grid and production profile. Before execution, save each alternative's source polygons, affected land-cover classes, transformation rules and decision question. Select alternatives that answer the appendix's future-impact question: which lands would have disproportionately large water-quality impacts if disturbed or developed, and what protection or management changes would avoid those impacts. The prime chooses the actual two alternatives; the workbench retains their explicit definitions. [RFP appendix, pages 8–9](https://www.upstateforever.org/files/files/20261002_FINAL_EC-SDC%20Mapping%20RFP%20APPENDIX%20COMBINED.pdf#page=8)

Keep native model outputs, run selections and logs together with the derived annual N/P/sediment mass rasters. Explain each reported sediment quantity using its actual native output: hillslope erosion and sediment delivered to waterways have different meanings. Nutrient mass, sediment mass and a combined planning index each retain their own units.

The scenario comparison and the paired-profile analysis use their own recorded direction labels. The production scenario comparison is **alternative minus baseline**; avoided export is the opposite sign. The paired-ensemble reduction is **baseline minus alternative**. Tables and maps carry the appropriate direction next to the quantity. Percentage and total comparisons identify their valid geographic support, so changes in coverage remain visible.

The combined priority is a declared, dimensionless weighted normalization of the separate physical components. Its weights, caps, common support and zero handling travel with the release. Drinking-water and high-quality-water priorities add the retained upstream relationship and resource designations. Parcel summaries identify covered area, total/area-normalized export or change as applicable, overlap treatment and actual source coverage. This gives the prime a quantitative basis for reviewing recommendations rather than an unexplained ranked map.
