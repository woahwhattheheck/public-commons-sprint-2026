# Web-hosting case method and operating handover

This is a usable method for the RFP's web-hosting lane. Its individual tasks, role minutes, direct costs, opening hours and personnel become case commitments only when entered from the actual provider and written programme-manager work order. The vendor submission compiler accepts the same service key, web-hosting.

## Intake and requested work

Record the beneficiary alias, communication arrangement, current site and critical functions, the operational problem, intended audience and the affected service. Keep identifying contacts and access material in the approved case custody surface. Enter actual staffing and operating hours; select the people who can perform the work and their actual approved labor categories. Preserve the full intake and source inventory in the local workbook.

Assess the current hosting/platform, source and database custody, domains and DNS, certificate management, backups, accessibility needs and applicable retention/privacy requirements. Record which systems the beneficiary actually owns or authorizes. For a static site, retain the complete source and original assets. For a dynamic system, record its actual application/runtime and database format, dependency versions and restore procedure. Include the required translation and coordination effort in the proposed tasks when it belongs to the approved casework.

The requested plan names concrete deliverables: a recoverable source snapshot, a restored copy in the selected environment, the real operating configuration, customer instructions and a receiving exercise. Budget role minutes by task and preapproved hosting/travel/other costs separately at their actual cost. Case application and proposal preparation remain excluded from billing. Submit the request and retain the original written work order before starting the approved casework.

## Migration and recovery

Retain an original source/database snapshot and confirm that the intended receiving environment can restore it. Record the source selection and the actual artifact identity in the work log. For a live transition, agree the actual cutover window and keep a recoverable prior configuration. Confirm the nameserver/record ownership and obtain the actual needed access through the authorized custodian; credentials stay out of the public source and ordinary case reports.

Install the selected source and retained configuration in the receiving environment. Exercise the public routes, essential forms, asset links, redirects, authentication boundaries and normal error handling that belong to the actual system. Inspect the rendered desktop and narrow views. Open the actual exported and downloadable files in their normal reader. Apply focused fixes to the real system. A static migration does not silently grow into forensics, independent security assessment or continuous incident response: enter a changed task/scope and obtain the actual approved amendment for additional work.

At cutover, record the actual DNS/configuration change and its time, certificate and redirect behavior, receiving outcome and any rollback decision. Preserve the source and operating instructions needed to repeat the deployment. Observe the real agreed service behavior over the case's stated window and record the result; the workbook does not invent ongoing support or 24/7 availability.

## Time, actual costs and change control

Record each worker's original task time with UTC start/end, actual loaded category and source log. Keep preparation and administrative effort explicitly classified. Correct a mistaken uninvoiced entry by retaining its original, recording a source-backed void and adding its correct replacement. Later scope approval preserves every earlier approval and the budget that was effective when the work happened. A future budget increase cannot make earlier excess work eligible.

Record each actual direct cost against the originally preapproved line with its complete receipt. Preserve the actual allowability determination and any relevant air-travel, equipment, subcontract or goods-origin context. The original Appendix A and actual agreement establish applicability. Apply the responsible person's determination to the specific procurement rather than treating all hosting work as the same legal case.

## Receiving, invoice and closeout

Prepare the engagement report from the actual work and original cost entries it covers. Include the delivered source/version, deployment and restore instructions, actual receiving result, remaining operational work and the agreed handover owner. Report selections explicitly name their time and expense entries; an earlier report cannot stand in for later work.

Prepare the invoice from the exact approved, performed entries. The application excludes preparation and already billed entries. Original prepared invoice bytes remain stable after subsequent payment observations. Record actual submission and payment from their original sources. At closeout, account for all active eligible entries through an invoice or an explicit writeoff and preserve the engagement report. The complete handover includes the current editable workbook, every original document, requested/approved scope history, source-bound reports and original invoices.

## Concrete integration with the vendor proposal

The maintained vendor compiler's services, laborRates, budget and qualification fields enter a separate proposal review through vendor-review.mjs. It calls the existing compiler's check/render functions and retains the exact original vendor file. Missing prices stay missing. The actual awarded contract supplies the later approved rates, category ceilings, contract dates and source documents. This preserves the useful draft-to-delivery relationship without turning a proposed rate or requested ceiling into an awarded term.

Use the actual provider's work examples, staffing, opening hours and source disclosure permission in the vendor submission. The application and authored receiving course demonstrate a software capability; they do not supply invented customer references or assigned personnel.
