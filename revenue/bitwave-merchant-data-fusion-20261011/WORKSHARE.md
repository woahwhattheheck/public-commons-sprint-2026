TokenJunkie Labs will configure and operate a complete merchant-to-accountant close workflow for one customer implementation. ## Included delivery unit

The engagement covers one agreed merchant export/report family, one customer Bitwave organization and one approved Data Fusion schema, **up to 5,000 source events in total across two monthly closes**, and **up to two corrected accountant-return cycles**. Each close is labeled and tied to its frozen source report or period-close snapshot.

We use the working merchant accountant application and its Bitwave module, configure the customer-specific source mapping and schema, execute the agreed capture and return workflow, resolve the source/return exceptions with their owners, and transfer the runnable source and operating environment.

The original records retain their exact native amount strings, asset labels and network classification. An accountant's external reference and status are separate fields with their own history. Several original events may share a legitimate batch-journal reference chosen by the accountant.

## Customer inputs and decision ownership

Provide one representative original export/report family and the two agreed close populations; the source field definitions and native units; a technical owner; authorized access to the selected Bitwave organization and Data Fusion capability; the desired data-source naming/schema; and a designated accountant with the actual external-account mapping and return expectations.

The source owner confirms the event population and report/period identity. The technical owner approves organization and schema setup. The accountant supplies account codes, external references, review status and interpretation, and accepts the resulting return. We implement and operate the source, reconciliation, correction and transfer process with those owners.

The project runs in the agreed customer-controlled environment. The source application uses Node.js and the existing local workspace. Customer credentials are supplied through the agreed runtime configuration; retained request and handoff artifacts contain the nonsecret configuration and source/result records.

## Eight-business-day delivery sequence

**Days 1–2 — Define the close and configure the source.** Confirm the two populations, field definitions, exact amount representation and review ownership. Normalize the agreed source family into the original merchant accountant handoff, inspect the original report/period bindings and resolve input ambiguities with the source owner. Establish the approved Bitwave schema and destination naming.

**Days 3–4 — Deliver and reconcile the first close.** Prepare the native data-source requests and row batches, execute the authorized customer delivery, retain its results and complete native row capture. Compare every returned source row with its original event and resolve missing, changed or duplicate records before reviewing bookkeeping assignments.

**Days 5–6 — Complete the return and repeat the close.** Preview and retain the designated accountant's external statuses, book references, account codes and notes. Process the agreed corrections and repeat the complete operation for the second monthly close. Up to two corrected returns are included across the engagement.

**Days 7–8 — Transfer an operable workflow.** Resolve the agreed acceptance items, transfer source/configuration and source-linked close packets, walk the recipient through the correction and history views, and reopen the complete backed-up workspace with them. Assign remaining operational follow-up to its named owner.

The schedule starts once the agreed source populations, authorized customer access, schema decisions and reviewers are available. Additional export families, organizations, periods, ERP interfaces or a continuing monthly operating service can be scoped against the actual workload.
