## The customer outcome

The product connects a merchant's useful API, dataset or tool to the buyer's actual application. A merchant selects its source and terms. A buyer approves a specific request, retains its wallet and spending authority, and receives the complete output. The same operation retains the transfer, delivery, fulfillment and accounting needed to continue after interruption or hand the installation to another engineer.

The funded outcome is **two independently operated merchants, two independent buyer operators, and the same useful service operating through two independently operated facilitators**. Each installation starts with an agreed customer task, an authorized source, an exact output and a receiving application. Its next purchase serves a fresh reporting period, changed source edition or distinct customer request.

For a dataset, the output includes every selected page and row. For a report or file, it includes the complete result and source edition. For a paid tool, it preserves the method, arguments and selected payment terms. The receiving engineer can reopen the acquired work and continue the same business task.

## What the award changes

The customer operation supplies the distinction from existing payment, discovery and SDK products. Native SDKs and selected facilitators provide verification and settlement. The proposed work preserves source meaning, the buyer's approved request, complete useful output and the operating state required by the customer.

Integration must keep types, null and missing values, keys, pagination, units, time basis and source-edition behavior intact. Payment review binds the selected network, asset, recipient and integer amount. An interrupted operation continues with its original financial and delivery state. A change of compatible facilitator preserves the same useful service and receiving task.

Compare the current implementation with maintained alternatives using their current source and documented behavior. The application should explain the remaining customer adaptation, recurring use and handover that each funded package delivers. Keep the market comparison in the original manuscript so reviewers can assess it alongside architecture, budget and outcomes.

RFP Track remains an alternative only when a specific active request and the proposed deliverables fit. The selected Open Track application proceeds on its own stated contribution and current issuer requirements. Integration Track has its own existing-product, building-block and outcome requirements; a track change should update the same application and delivery model.

## Six prospective deliverables

Day zero is actual award acceptance. The planning rate is **$95 per hour**.

| Tranche and window | Deliverable | Hours | Cost | Completion |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| T1, days 1–30 | Merchant-resource integration | 80 | $7,600 | Both independent merchants operate the selected HTTP or MCP service; the buyer opens the complete output in its receiving application. |
| T1, days 1–30 | Authority and handover | 40 | $3,800 | Another accountable operator reopens each installation and completes the agreed source or terms change, retaining earlier acquired output. |
| T2, days 31–90 | Provider and ledger deployment | 70 | $6,650 | The same useful operation completes through two independent facilitators. Two independent buyers complete at least ten useful testnet purchases during seven consecutive days ending by day 90. |
| T2, days 31–90 | Deployed threat response | 50 | $4,750 | The installed service handles an interrupted settled purchase and a merchant-delivery failure through notification, acknowledgement, recovery and complete receiving. |
| T3, days 91–150 | Mainnet rollout | 90 | $8,550 | Both merchants operate on mainnet. Two independent buyers complete at least thirty useful purchases during fourteen consecutive days ending by day 150, with each buyer returning on another day. |
| T3, days 91–150 | Production recovery and handover | 70 | $6,650 | A receiving engineer restores state and customer files, reconciles subsequent operations, measures recovery time and completes a support case. |
| **Total** | **Reusable product improvements and two independently operated installations** | **400** | **$38,000** | **Complete customer operations and maintainable operating state.** |

The implementation costs are **$11,400 / $11,400 / $15,200** across T1/T2/T3. The standard four award payments are **$3,800 / $7,600 / $11,400 / $15,200** at acceptance and the subsequent approved milestones. Maintain these as separate cost and cash-receipt schedules in SF47.

SCF pays the USD-equivalent award value in XLM under its stated pricing mechanism. Its current budget guidance uses the **10% / 20% / 30% / 40%** payment structure and completion-form timing measured from the previous received tranche. Use actual receipt dates and any issuer-confirmed amendment in the original grant case. ## Customer use and measurement

A completed purchase joins a distinct approved intent, its native transfer and complete useful output. Keep the network and asset explicit, with fees recorded separately. Preserve attempted, completed, partial, canceled, refunded and unresolved operations within the observation period.

Count independent operators by their accountable customer or operator identity. Multiple wallets or processes belonging to one operator share that identity. Reopening an acquired result or resuming its download continues the original purchase. A returning buyer completes a distinct useful purchase on another day.

The two-facilitator outcome uses the same useful service through both operators. Preserve the approved resource, request, payment selection and receiving task across that change. A listing in each of two unrelated catalogues does not satisfy the shared-service delivery criterion.

For each customer, retain the task definition, selected source population or period, useful output, active effort, corrections and receiving acceptance. Where a previous operating method is available, compare that same task and input definition. Customer adoption should produce useful repeated work and a service its engineer can maintain.

## Operating response

Apply the installed threat model to the selected source edition, merchant and buyer authority, network, facilitator and protected records. Name the notification destination and primary and backup responders. Configure the installation's reviewed age, rate and capacity thresholds.

An observed financial or output contradiction opens the corresponding incident. An uncertain payment is reconciled through its original intent. A delivery interruption retains the settled outcome and unfinished step. The receiving engineer must be able to find that same incident, acknowledgement, remediation and useful result after restart or restoration.

The existing threat-response document supplies the detailed scenarios and monitoring contract. The funded T2 package applies and improves those behaviors on the two selected installations.

## Commercial adoption

Continue authorized new-prospect research and outreach through the [existing adoption workflow](./SF49_ADOPTION_PILOT_PLAYBOOK_20261009.md). Start with a public customer need or a service whose useful operation has an identifiable buyer. Prepare the source, implementation and customer receiving work that makes the offer concrete.

The original private relationship record owns the sender, correspondence, current scope and any explicit owner contact restriction. A favorable human reply routes that relationship to Bryce for subsequent sends while internal engineering continues. Keep new-prospect discovery and delivery for other relationships moving through their own records.

Sell a specific implementation and handover, with separately scoped care where useful. The workorder identifies the commissioning payer, resource merchant, independent buyer, receiving engineer, agreed output, price, acceptance and support responsibility. Grant-funded reusable work and separately purchased integration retain their own assignments and accounting.
