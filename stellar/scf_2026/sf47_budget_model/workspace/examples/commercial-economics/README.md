It joins the original proposed implementation and care terms to a review of complete-output pricing, underlying requests, retries, full-price refunds and monthly operating costs. It creates planning files only. It does not write an operating case, change the grant budget, create payments, assert external adoption or record cash.

## Complete output, requests and charge units

Inputs intentionally leave the buyer, merchant and facilitator unassigned. Native payment asset, price and USD tariff remain unpriced. The original engine therefore reports zero paid purchases and zero independent purchase opportunities. No installation or customer acceptance is implied.

The illustrative workload uses a thirty-day billing period beginning January 4, 2027 and the unchanged fourteen-day calculation window. It assumes 1, 2 or 5 useful outputs per day and four original requests per output. These are editable workload assumptions, not sales forecasts or observed demand.

For each workload the same original model evaluates:

- **Complete-output billing:** one original funding group per output.
- **Original-request billing:** four original funding groups per output.
- **Retry/refund sensitivity:** one additional underlying request per output, with no new funding group, and every tenth output fully refunded. In request billing, all four original charges for that output are refunded.

Every output remains one task. The additional request represents work on that same task. Refund sensitivity retains the original charges, original fees and incurred work; it changes the retained-revenue denominator separately. The original planning model does not claim those refunds occurred. Actual refunds belong in the original Commercial reconciliation workflow with their native cash/intent records.

## Exact contribution coefficients

Let:

- `N` be complete outputs and `R` original requests per output;
- `A` be additional retry requests per output;
- `B` be original charges: `N` for output billing or `N × R` for request billing;
- `J` be fully refunded charge equivalents;
- `D/M` be calculation days divided by billing-period days;
- `F` be monthly fixed cost, including purchased care and any additional fixed hosting/support;
- `c` be provider/network cost per attempted request, `p` a nonrefundable fee per original charge, and `u` handling cost per refunded charge;
- `h × w` be actual cash labor cost per complete output, excluding already purchased care.

The zero-margin gross quote per original charge is:

`[(D/M) × F + N × (R+A) × c + B × p + J × u + N × h × w] / (B−J)`

The output retains each coefficient as an exact numerator and denominator. It does not round unknown fees into zero, select a customer tariff or convert native token amounts. Percentage-based fees, taxes or other cost rules require their own actual term. A margin objective belongs in the same reviewed service plan after its whole cost basis is entered. A workload with no retained charge equivalents cannot recover positive cost through those charges.

For the conditional purchased-care cost alone, `600 × 14/30 = 280` is allocated to the fourteen-day period:

| Outputs in fourteen days | Original requests | Care-only floor per output | Care-only floor per original request |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | 56 | $20 | $5 |
| 28 | 112 | $10 | $2.50 |
| 70 | 280 | $4 | $1 |

These are cost-allocation lower bounds with zero contribution from all other cost terms. They are not complete break-even quotes. Every original full report keeps provider, fee and cash-labor coverage incomplete until entered.

At fourteen outputs with one extra request each, there are seventy attempts and fourteen original output charges. Refunding one output leaves thirteen charge equivalents. The care-only floor becomes exactly `280/13` dollars per original output charge; the attempt-cost coefficient is `70/13`, the original-payment-fee coefficient is `14/13`, and the refund-handling coefficient is `1/13`. Original-request billing retains fifty-six original charges, refunds four, and uses fifty-two equivalents. The same incurred work is retained.

**Period allocation is not cash timing.** Allocating $280 to this analysis does not reduce a full $600 monthly care invoice or move its payment date. The cash forecast retains the actual full invoice amount and its actual due/collection date.
